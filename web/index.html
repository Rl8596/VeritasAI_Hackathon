Limits you should know about

  • Research demo. Not a medical device. Not for diagnosis. A student project that no authority has checked. It can only suggest which X-rays to look at first. The decision stays with a clinician.
  • A model can be very accurate on its own test X-rays and still be wrong for a patient unlike the ones it learned from. The two checks catch many such cases, not all. No warning does not mean the result is right.
  • The pneumonia score is not the chance that the patient has pneumonia. Each model flags above its own cut-off, chosen before testing so that it catches 95% of the pneumonia cases in its validation X-rays. That is why the two cut-offs are so different.
  • The models know two answers, pneumonia or not. In our test, adults who were sick with something else were often flagged as well: 42% of them by the children-only model and 57% by the children + adults model.
  • For the adult X-rays, "pneumonia" means that radiologists marked a lung opacity. That is a finding on the picture, not a confirmed diagnosis.
  • The models learned from public X-rays of two groups only: children aged 1 to 5 from one hospital in Guangzhou, China, and (second model) adults from one hospital in the USA. Babies, older children, teenagers, other hospitals and other X-ray machines are untested.
  • The heatmap shows what influenced the model. It is coarse (a 7 by 7 grid, smoothed) and it is not a map of the disease.
  • Please do not use pictures that show a patient's name or other personal details.
